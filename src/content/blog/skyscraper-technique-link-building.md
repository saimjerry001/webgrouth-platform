**The skyscraper technique has three steps: find content that already attracts a lot of links, create something genuinely better, then reach out to the sites linking to the original and suggest yours as an upgrade.** It's one of the most widely taught link building methods, and it still works — but the bar for "genuinely better" has risen considerably since the technique was first popularized.

## Why the basic version underperforms now

The original pitch to site owners — "I made something similar but bigger" — has been sent to most active resource-page maintainers many times over. A skyscraper piece that's simply longer than the original, without being more useful, rarely earns replies anymore. Site owners have seen the pattern and can usually tell when "better" just means "padded."

Genuinely improving on existing top content now requires a real, checkable upgrade: newer data, a missing angle the original doesn't cover, better organization, or a practical tool the original lacks entirely.

## Finding content worth outranking

1. **Search your target keyword and identify pages with strong backlink counts** relative to their competitors — these are proven link magnets, not just ranking pages.
2. **Read the top 3-5 results critically.** What's missing? What's outdated? What do the comments (where visible) ask for that the article doesn't answer?
3. **Check the publish or last-updated date.** Content that hasn't been refreshed in a couple of years is often the easiest to genuinely improve on, simply by being current. See our [content refresh guide](/blog/content-refresh-seo) for how to identify and prioritize this kind of opportunity in your own content too.

## What "genuinely better" actually looks like

| Weak upgrade | Real upgrade |
| --- | --- |
| Same points, more words | New examples, data, or a distinct angle the original lacks |
| A longer list with no more depth per item | Fewer, better-explained items with real detail |
| Generic advice restated | An interactive tool, template, or calculator readers can actually use |

If your niche allows it, building a free interactive tool related to the topic is one of the more durable "upgrade" angles, since it's genuinely hard for competitors to copy quickly and gives outreach targets something concrete and novel to link to.

## The outreach step people skip

Creating the better piece is only half the technique — the outreach to sites currently linking to the original is what actually generates new links. Export the backlink list for the page you're outranking (see our [competitor backlink analysis guide](/blog/competitor-backlink-analysis-guide) for the filtering process), then pitch site owners individually, referencing the specific page they linked to and what your version adds.

A generic mass email referencing "the article you linked to" without naming it specifically reads as spam and gets ignored. Personalize each pitch to the actual page and actual outdated or missing element you're addressing.

## A realistic response rate expectation

Skyscraper outreach typically has a modest response and conversion rate even when done well — site owners have limited incentive to update a working link unless your replacement is clearly better and easy to verify. Treat it as a volume tactic requiring many genuine, individually-checked opportunities, not a guaranteed-reply template.

## Where this fits with guest posting

Skyscraper content and guest posting solve different problems. Skyscraper content earns links to a piece already living on your site; guest posting places new content (and a link) on someone else's site. They complement each other well — a strong skyscraper piece on your own site becomes exactly the kind of resource worth referencing from within a guest post you write elsewhere. ## Common questions

### How long does a skyscraper campaign typically take to show results?

Building the content, running outreach, and seeing meaningful new links accumulate typically spans several weeks to a few months, depending on outreach volume and niche responsiveness. Treat it as an ongoing content investment, not a quick win.

### Do I need the absolute best content in the niche to succeed?

Not the single best in an absolute sense — you need to be clearly better than the specific page you're targeting for outreach, on the dimension that page is weakest in. A narrower, well-executed improvement often outperforms a vague attempt at "the best guide ever."

### Should I always make my skyscraper content longer?

No — length is a byproduct of genuinely covering more ground, not a goal itself. Some effective upgrades are actually shorter and more focused than the original, if the original was bloated.

### Is skyscraper outreach different from broken link building?

Yes — skyscraper outreach targets links to content that's still live but outdated or weaker than yours; [broken link building](/blog/broken-link-building-guide) targets links that are already dead. Both use a similar "here's a better/working option" pitch structure but different triggers.
