**Two websites with near-identical DA, traffic and niche can charge $60 and $600 for the same type of guest post placement, and both prices can be legitimate.** The gap usually comes down to a handful of specific factors, not arbitrary markup — though markup exists too, and it helps to know how to spot it.

## The factors that legitimately affect price

**Traffic quality, not just volume.** A site with 5,000 monthly visitors who are genuinely searching for topics in your niche can reasonably charge more than a site with 50,000 visitors from unrelated, low-intent traffic. Ask what the traffic actually consists of, not just the total number.

**Editorial effort.** A publisher who actually reads submissions, requests revisions, and maintains quality standards is providing a service beyond just hosting a link. That review process costs the publisher time, and the price often reflects it.

**Niche scarcity.** In a crowded niche like general marketing, guest post inventory is abundant and competitive, which pushes prices down. In a narrow, less-served niche, fewer publishers means less price competition.

**Content requirements.** If the site requires original, well-researched content of a minimum length with specific formatting, and rejects thin submissions, that quality bar itself justifies a higher price than a site that will publish nearly anything.

**Link policy.** Sites that limit outbound links per article, require topical relevance, or cap the number of paid placements per month are managing their own link profile carefully — which is exactly the kind of site whose links carry more weight, and whose owners know it.

## Where markup shows up

Not every price difference reflects genuine value. A few patterns worth watching for:

- **A price with no relationship to the site's actual metrics.** If a DR 15 site with negligible traffic quotes the same price as a DR 45 site with real traffic, ask what specifically justifies it.
- **A "bundle" price that hides multiple hidden fees** — writing, formatting, "guaranteed indexing," rush processing — added on top of a headline number that looked reasonable at first glance.
- **Pricing that changes based on your perceived budget** rather than a fixed rate card. This is hard to prove but worth noting if a quote moves significantly after you mention your budget out loud.

## What's usually included, and what isn't

| Typically included | Often billed separately |
| --- | --- |
| Placement / publishing fee | Original article writing |
| Basic editorial review | Custom images beyond a stock cover photo |
| One dofollow or nofollow link (per the site's policy) | Extra links beyond the standard allowance |
| Live for as long as the publisher's standard terms state | "Permanent" or "lifetime" guarantees beyond normal terms |

Confirm this list explicitly before paying. A price that looks cheap can turn out costlier once you add the writing fee the publisher assumed you already knew about.

## Common questions

### Is a cheap guest post ($20-40) ever worth it?

Sometimes, if the site has genuine relevant traffic and a real editorial process — cheap doesn't automatically mean low quality. But verify it the same way you would a $300 site: check traffic, read recent articles, and confirm the link and content terms in writing.

### Why do some sites charge more for a dofollow link than a nofollow one?

Because a dofollow link is understood to pass more ranking value in most SEO frameworks, publishers who separate the two are pricing for the specific value being requested. See our guide to [dofollow, nofollow, sponsored and UGC links](/blog/dofollow-nofollow-sponsored-ugc-links) for what each attribute actually does.

### Should I ever negotiate guest post pricing?

It's common practice, especially for bulk orders or ongoing relationships. Approach it transparently — ask directly whether there's flexibility for volume or a recurring arrangement, rather than trying to pressure a single one-off price down.

### Does a higher price guarantee a better placement?

No. Price correlates loosely with quality but isn't proof of it. Always verify the site directly using our [guide to vetting guest post sites](/blog/how-to-vet-guest-post-sites) regardless of what it costs.
