**Guest post marketplaces sell you speed and predictability; manual outreach trades that speed for lower cost and, often, safer, more genuinely earned links.** Neither is universally better — the right choice depends on your budget, timeline, and how much control you want over exactly where your links land.

## How marketplaces actually work

A guest post marketplace lists websites with published prices, typical turnaround times, and stated requirements. You browse, pick a site, pay, and (usually) submit or commission content through the platform. It's essentially a directory of publishers who have pre-agreed to sell placements, aggregated for convenience.

**Advantages:** speed, predictable pricing, less relationship-building overhead, easier to scale a defined monthly volume.

**Trade-offs:** marketplace inventory sometimes includes sites that exist mainly to sell links rather than run genuine editorial operations — vetting quality still matters, marketplace listing alone isn't proof of a good site. Prices can also carry a platform markup compared to a direct relationship with the same publisher.

## How manual outreach actually works

Manual outreach means researching relevant publications yourself, pitching editors directly, and building a placement through the publication's normal contributor process — the process covered in our [guest post outreach guide](/blog/guest-post-outreach).

**Advantages:** typically cheaper per placement (no platform fee), often results in more genuinely editorial, higher-trust placements, and builds relationships that can lead to repeat, informal future opportunities.

**Trade-offs:** slower, less predictable, requires more skill in pitching and more tolerance for rejection, and doesn't scale linearly with just more budget — it scales with more time and outreach skill.

## A comparison to help decide

| | Marketplaces | Manual outreach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to first placement | Fast (days) | Slower (weeks) |
| Predictability | High — known price, known site | Variable — depends on response rate |
| Typical cost per placement | Often higher, includes platform fee | Often lower, but requires time investment |
| Vetting still required? | Yes — listing doesn't guarantee quality | Yes — but you're choosing the target yourself |
| Scalability | Easy to scale with budget | Scales with outreach capacity, not just budget |
| Relationship building | Minimal | Can lead to ongoing, informal opportunities |

## Vetting still matters either way

Whichever route you use, apply the same checks before committing: real traffic, genuine editorial process, relevant audience, and reasonable link and content policies. Our [guide to vetting guest post sites](/blog/how-to-vet-guest-post-sites) applies equally to a marketplace listing and a site you found through direct research — a marketplace's own quality control varies and shouldn't replace your own check.

## A practical hybrid approach

Many successful campaigns use both: marketplaces (or a vetted publisher network) for predictable monthly volume and faster turnaround, combined with ongoing manual outreach to relationship-build with a smaller set of especially strong, relevant publications that aren't available through any marketplace. This gives you both the scale of a platform and the quality ceiling of hand-picked relationships.

## Common questions

### Are guest post marketplaces against Google's guidelines?

Buying links specifically to manipulate rankings, regardless of the sales channel, is against Google's [link scheme guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). The safer approach, on any channel, is to prioritize genuinely relevant, quality placements and use appropriate link attributes (`sponsored` or `nofollow`) for paid links, as covered in our [dofollow, nofollow, sponsored and UGC guide](/blog/dofollow-nofollow-sponsored-ugc-links).

### Is manual outreach always cheaper?

Usually cheaper in direct cost, but factor in the time cost of research, pitching, and follow-up — for a small team without dedicated outreach capacity, that time cost can outweigh the platform fee a marketplace or managed network charges.

### Can I switch between the two approaches over time?

Yes — many campaigns start with a marketplace or managed network for quick initial volume, then shift more effort toward manual outreach and relationship-building as the campaign matures and specific high-value targets become clear.

### Do marketplaces offer better guarantees than manual outreach?

Some do offer republishing or replacement guarantees if a placement is taken down — worth checking the specific terms, since this varies significantly between platforms and isn't standard everywhere.
