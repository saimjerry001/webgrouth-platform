**A brand-new website has no link history, no authority, and no track record — which means both the tactics available to it and the pace it should move at are different from an established site's.** Jumping straight into a large guest posting campaign before laying the groundwork often wastes budget on links a still-empty site can't yet make good use of.

## Why sequence matters more than speed for a new site

Links point to something — and if that something is thin, unfinished, or badly structured, the link's value is capped regardless of the linking site's quality. The first priority for a new site isn't link volume; it's making sure there's a genuinely strong page worth linking to.

## A realistic first-year sequence

**Months 1-2: foundation.** Finish core pages, make sure your site's own internal linking is solid (see our [internal linking guide](/blog/internal-linking-seo)), and publish a handful of genuinely strong, comprehensive pieces of content — the pages you'll actually want links pointing to later.

**Months 2-4: easy, low-risk links.** Business directories relevant to your industry, your own social profiles, local citations if applicable, and any partner or supplier relationships that naturally warrant a mention. These build a baseline footprint without requiring outreach skill or budget yet.

**Months 3-6: first guest posting and outreach.** Start guest post outreach at a modest, sustainable pace — a handful of placements per month rather than an aggressive volume, per our guide on [safe link building velocity](/blog/safe-link-building-velocity). Prioritize genuine relevance over pure authority score at this stage; a new site benefits more from a handful of well-matched, real placements than a burst of generic ones.

**Months 6-12: scale what's working, diversify.** By this point you'll have data on which content and which types of placements are actually moving rankings — see our [guide to measuring guest posting results](/blog/measure-guest-posting-results). Scale the channels that are working, and add diversity: journalist outreach, resource page mentions, broken link building, alongside continued guest posting.

## What to avoid in the first few months

- **A large batch of purchased links from unrelated, low-quality sites**, hoping to "jump start" authority. This is close to the exact pattern search engines are built to recognize as manipulative, and it's especially risky with no other signals (age, brand mentions, real traffic) to offset it.
- **Exact-match anchor text on most early links.** With so few total links, an early heavy skew toward one exact keyword phrase is highly visible in your profile. Check your accumulating mix with the [anchor text ratio analyzer](/tools/anchor-text-ratio-analyzer) from the very first links onward.
- **Ignoring your own content quality** while chasing external links. A new site with ten mediocre links and a thin homepage is worse off than one with three great links and a genuinely strong, complete core site.

## Budgeting realistically for year one

New sites often underestimate how much of a link building budget should go toward content creation rather than placement fees in the early months — a strong page worth linking to is a prerequisite, not an afterthought. ## Common questions

### How long before a new site sees ranking movement from link building?

Commonly several months at minimum, and often longer for competitive keywords — new sites also face a general trust-building period with search engines independent of link building specifically. Patience and consistent execution matter more than speed here.

### Should a brand-new site avoid guest posting entirely at first?

Not entirely — a small number of genuinely relevant, well-matched placements early on is fine and often helpful. The caution is against large volume before the site has content and structure worth linking to.

### Is it worth paying for a "starter package" of many cheap links?

Generally not recommended — a large batch of low-quality links early, before a site has any other trust signals, is closer to the risky pattern search engines flag than to genuine, gradual authority building.

### What's the single highest-priority link building action in month one?

Usually none — month one is better spent finishing the site's own content and structure. The first genuinely valuable outreach usually starts once there's a page worth someone else linking to.
