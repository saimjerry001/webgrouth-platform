**There's no official "links per month" limit Google publishes, and link velocity isn't a fixed rule — but a sudden, large spike in new links on a site with no prior link-building history is a pattern that draws more scrutiny than the same total number of links spread naturally over time.** The concern isn't speed itself; it's whether the pattern looks like something a real, growing business would produce.

## Why pace matters more than total volume

A well-established site with years of organic mentions, press coverage, and guest post placements can absorb a large batch of new links without looking unusual — it already has a track record of attracting links at some pace. A brand-new site jumping from zero to fifty new links in a month has no such history, and that specific pattern — sudden, large, and often concentrated in similar link types or anchor text — is closer to what a manipulative campaign looks like than what organic growth looks like.

Google's [guidance on link spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) doesn't specify a velocity threshold, but it does describe large-scale, patterned link acquisition as a signal its systems are built to recognize. Velocity is one input into that pattern recognition, alongside anchor text uniformity, link source quality, and content originality.

## What a natural pace typically looks like

Genuine, organic link growth tends to be:

- **Irregular**, not perfectly steady — some weeks produce several new links (a piece goes viral, a PR win lands), others produce none.
- **Diverse in source type** — a mix of guest posts, mentions, directory listings, and occasional press, rather than one link type repeated identically.
- **Correlated with actual activity** — a launch, a piece of content that performed well, a press mention — rather than links appearing with no corresponding event.

A manufactured pattern tends to be the opposite: unnaturally steady, concentrated in one link type, and disconnected from any actual business activity.

## A practical pacing approach for guest posting campaigns

If you're running a deliberate guest post campaign (which is a legitimate, common practice, distinct from a manipulative link scheme), a few practical habits keep the pace looking reasonable:

1. **Scale gradually on a new or low-authority site.** Start with a handful of placements per month and increase over time as the site's own profile grows, rather than launching at full planned volume immediately.
2. **Vary anchor text naturally.** Check your accumulated mix with the [anchor text ratio analyzer](/tools/anchor-text-ratio-analyzer) periodically — a heavy concentration of exact-match anchors is a stronger red flag than raw link count.
3. **Diversify placement types.** Mix guest posts, link insertions, and other channels like [journalist outreach](/blog/haro-alternatives-journalist-outreach) rather than relying on one identical tactic repeated at volume.
4. **Space out a genuinely large campaign** across months rather than weeks, particularly for a newer site with limited existing link history.

## When a fast pace is genuinely fine

A large, real news event — a funding announcement, a major product launch, viral content — can legitimately generate many links in a short window, and this is not the same risk pattern as manufactured link velocity. The distinguishing factor is always whether there's a real, checkable reason for the spike, and whether the links themselves are genuinely earned rather than purchased or templated.

## Tracking pace over time

Log placement dates in your [campaign tracker](/tools/guest-post-tracker-template) and periodically review the monthly distribution, not just the running total. A cumulative count can look fine while the underlying monthly pattern is actually quite spiky — reviewing it by month surfaces that in a way a single total number doesn't.

## Common questions

### Is there an official maximum number of links per month?

No, Google has not published a specific numeric threshold. Pace is evaluated as part of a broader pattern, not against a fixed limit.

### Does link velocity matter for an established, high-authority site?

Less than for a new site, but a genuinely unusual and sudden pattern can still draw scrutiny regardless of site age, especially if paired with other spam signals like unnatural anchor text.

### Can I speed up link building if I have a bigger budget this quarter?

You can increase volume, but do it by diversifying placement types and sources rather than dramatically increasing the pace of one identical tactic — that diversification is what keeps a larger campaign looking natural.

### Should I space out link building evenly, like one link per week?

Perfectly even spacing can itself look unnatural, since real link acquisition is typically irregular. Aim for a realistic, somewhat uneven pace rather than a mechanically consistent one.
