**Almost nobody links to a product page directly, on purpose — link building for e-commerce mostly works by earning links elsewhere on the site, then letting internal linking carry authority to the commercial pages that actually need to rank.** Understanding this distinction changes where you should spend outreach effort.

## Why product pages are the wrong target

A product or category page exists to sell something. It's rarely the kind of content another site owner wants to reference — there's no story, data, or unique insight in "Buy the XR400 Blender." Pitching outreach directly for a link to a product page almost always fails, because you're asking for a link to content with no independent reason to exist for anyone but a buyer.

The pages that actually attract links are informational: buying guides, comparison content, original research, tools, and genuinely useful how-to content related to your products.

## The two-layer strategy that actually works

1. **Build linkable informational content** — buying guides, care and maintenance guides, comparison pages, original surveys or data about your product category, seasonal guides. This is the content you pitch to other sites and publications.
2. **Link from that content to your commercial pages internally**, using relevant, natural anchor text. A blender buying guide should link to your actual blender category and specific popular models where it makes sense in context.

This way, external links accumulate on content designed to earn them, and internal linking carries a portion of that authority to the pages that actually convert. See our [internal linking guide](/blog/internal-linking-seo) for the mechanics of doing this well rather than just adding links everywhere.

## Content types that work particularly well for product-adjacent niches

- **Original data or surveys** about how customers use your product category — genuinely citable by journalists and bloggers covering the space.
- **Comparison and "vs" content** comparing your products (or product types generally) to alternatives, which naturally attracts links from readers researching a purchase.
- **Seasonal and gift guides**, which many publications actively seek content or product suggestions for, especially around major shopping periods.
- **Sustainability, sourcing, or manufacturing transparency content**, if genuinely relevant to your product — increasingly a topic journalists and bloggers cover with real interest.

## Digital PR angles specific to e-commerce

E-commerce brands often have access to genuinely newsworthy data other niches don't — actual sales trends, search behavior on-site, customer survey results. Packaging this into a simple, citable piece of original research (even a small one) is one of the more effective ways to earn links from press and industry publications. ## Guest posting for e-commerce brands

Guest posting still works for e-commerce, typically through:

- **Industry and trade publications** relevant to your product category (not general marketing blogs unrelated to what you sell).
- **Lifestyle and niche interest publications** whose audience genuinely overlaps with your buyers.
- **Comparison and review-focused sites**, where a relevant, non-promotional contribution can work well if the site allows it.

Match your pitch's angle to informational value, not to your product — an editor is far more likely to accept "how to choose the right blender for a small kitchen" than "why our blender is the best."

## Common questions

### Should I ever pay for a link directly to a product page?

Generally avoid it — beyond the practical difficulty of finding a legitimate publisher willing to do this naturally, links to purely commercial pages from unrelated content are a recognizable pattern that carries more risk than benefit. Build informational content instead and let internal linking do the work.

### How much content do I need before link building makes sense?

Enough genuinely useful, linkable informational content to give outreach something worth pitching — a handful of strong buying guides or comparison pages is a reasonable starting point before investing heavily in outreach.

### Do e-commerce sites need a different backlink profile than other business types?

Not fundamentally — the same principles (relevance, real traffic, real editorial process) apply. The main difference is simply that the content earning the links usually can't be the commercial pages themselves.

### Is guest posting worth it for a small e-commerce store?

Yes, if matched to genuinely relevant niche or lifestyle publications your actual buyers read — it doesn't require the scale of a major brand to be effective, as long as the content and targeting are done well.
