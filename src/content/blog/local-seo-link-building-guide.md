**A link from a small, genuinely local news site or community organization in your city can outperform a link from a much bigger national site with no geographic connection to your business.** Local SEO link building runs on a different logic than general link building — relevance to place and community matters as much as raw authority.

## Why generic high-authority links underperform locally

A national marketing blog linking to a plumbing company in one city tells search engines very little about that business's relevance to local searches. A link from the city's chamber of commerce, a local news outlet's business section, or a neighborhood association's resource page tells a much clearer geographic and community relevance story — even from a domain with a modest overall authority score.

This is the main reason a local business shouldn't apply the same link-target criteria as an e-commerce or SaaS company. Relevance to a place, not just to a topic, is the deciding factor.

## Where local links actually come from

- **Local news and community sites.** Being featured or quoted in local news coverage — openings, sponsorships, community involvement, awards — earns genuinely relevant links that are hard for competitors outside your area to replicate.
- **Local business associations and chambers of commerce.** Membership often comes with a directory listing and link, and it's a legitimate, easy-to-obtain local signal.
- **Local sponsorships.** Sponsoring a school event, sports team, or community fundraiser frequently comes with a link from the organizer's website — worth asking about explicitly if it isn't automatic.
- **Local supplier and partner pages.** If you work with other local businesses, ask whether a mutual "our partners" or "who we work with" page makes sense on both sites.
- **Guest content on locally-focused publications.** Regional lifestyle, business, or industry publications that serve your specific area function much like general guest posting, but with the added local relevance factor. Our general [guide to vetting guest post sites](/blog/how-to-vet-guest-post-sites) applies here too, with an added check on whether the publication's actual audience is local.

## The local citation, not just the link

Local SEO also involves citations — mentions of your business name, address and phone number (NAP) on directories, even without a link. Consistency across these citations (Google Business Profile, Yelp, industry-specific directories) supports local rankings independently of traditional backlink value. Treat citation consistency as a parallel, complementary workstream to link building, not a substitute for it.

## A simple prioritization framework

| Opportunity type | Relative effort | Typical local relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chamber of commerce / business association | Low | High |
| Local news feature or mention | Medium-high | Very high |
| Sponsorship-based link | Low-medium | High |
| Local guest post placement | Medium | High |
| Generic national directory | Low | Low |

Prioritize the top rows before spending effort on generic national directories, which have become less impactful for local rankings as search engines have gotten better at weighting genuinely local signals.

## Common questions

### Does a business need to be physically located in a city to get local links there?

Usually yes, in a practical sense — most local link opportunities (chambers, sponsorships, local news) expect a genuine local presence, and claiming one you don't have risks credibility problems beyond just SEO.

### How many local links does a small business actually need?

There's no fixed number — see our broader framework on [how many backlinks you need](/blog/how-many-backlinks-do-you-need) for how to estimate based on your specific local competition, rather than a generic target.

### Do local links need to be dofollow to help?

A mix is fine and realistic — many local directories and news sites apply nofollow by default. The visibility, referral traffic, and community trust from a local mention have value even when the link itself doesn't pass full ranking credit.

### Is local link building different for a multi-location business?

Yes — each location typically needs its own local relevance signals (its own local links, its own citations) rather than relying on the company's single headquarters location to cover every market it serves.
